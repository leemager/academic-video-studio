# Direct the video in ordinary language

Give Codex the audience, learning or communication purpose, approximate length, source folder and any visual constraints. It should take care of the technical details.

## Start from sources

“I've added my lecture notes in notes/introduction. Draft a five-minute outline for students who know nothing about this topic. Separate what the video should teach from what we'll discuss in class.”

“Turn my paper in notes/research into an explainer for colleagues outside the field. Include the research question, approach, main finding and limitations. Don't invent missing evidence.”

“Use this approved script exactly. Create a draft video using my voice. You may normalise numbers for pronunciation but keep the source text and captions accurate.”

## Review the draft

“The opening is too abstract. Start with the worked example, then explain the concept.”

“The diagram is doing too much. Show the three stages one at a time.”

“Keep the layout, but remove the small labels and corner headings.”

“The word at 1:20 sounds oddly stressed. The contrast is on the previous word. Give me two complete-sentence alternatives in the audition page.”

“Take B sounds like me. Apply that exact audio and preserve every other approved passage.”

“The pacing is too fast. Try a slightly slower whole-passage generation; don't insert silences between individual words or sentences.”

## Finish and reuse

“Export the final 1080p MP4, matching subtitles, source list and editable project. Keep the previous version.”

“Make a new version for next year's cohort. Tell me which facts need rechecking; preserve the rest of the script and approved narration.”

“Translate this approved short explanation into French. Keep the English source beside the translation for review, use my original voice reference, and flag pronunciation for a fluent speaker to check.”
